What runs here

        
The firmware is real. The screen is the Degen Walkie firmware, written in C with LVGL, compiled to WebAssembly from the same source that builds the ESP32-S3 image. The UI, the order tickets, the venue clients and the signing code are the code that ships on the device.

        
Signing is real. The device makes its own trading keys. Hyperliquid orders are signed with EIP-712 over a msgpack hash, exactly as Hyperliquid expects. Pacifica requests are signed with Ed25519 over sorted JSON. Watch the Network tab: each signed request shows how the server verified it.

        
The servers are mocks. Hyperliquid, Pacifica and Lighter are simulated in this page with their real endpoints and payload shapes. No real money moves. Prices start from Hyperliquid's public prices when your browser can reach them, then follow a random walk. Rooms, news and the AI answers are scripted.

        
Known gaps. Lighter signs with Schnorr over Poseidon2; that signer is not ported yet, so the firmware sends an Ed25519 stand-in that the mock accepts. The AI gives preset answers built from your live positions. Voice uses text you type, or your browser's speech recognition.
